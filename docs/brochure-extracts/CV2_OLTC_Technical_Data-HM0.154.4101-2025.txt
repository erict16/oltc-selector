1. General
The CV2 type oil-immersed vacuum on-load tap-changer (hereinafter referred to as "OLTC") is an improved 
vacuum composite product, suitable for oil-immersed transformers with a maximum system voltage of 40.5 kV to 
145 kV, a maximum rated through-current of 600 A, and a rated frequency of 50 Hz or 60 Hz. It complies with the 
requirements of GB/T 10230.1-2019 and IEC 60214-1:2014, "Tap-changers – Part 1: Performance requirements 
and test methods."
The OLTC is mounted on the transformer tank cover via a mounting flange.
The OLTC is driven by a motor drive unit (MDU). It connects to the MDU via a Top gearbox, a Driving shaft, and a 
Bevel gearbox, enabling manual or electric tap-changing operations, either locally or remotely.
The OLTC is available in three connection types: three-phase wye (star) neutral, three-phase delta, and single-
phase. The basic connection diagrams are shown in Figure 1-1:

4. Technical Performance Parameters
4.1	 Rated Step Capacity (PStn)
Step capacity is defined as the product of the step voltage and the load current, i.e., Ps = UI.  Rated step capacity 
is the maximum step capacity of the OLTC under continuous operation, i.e., PStn = Iux × Ui. See Figure 4-1 for 
rated step capacities.
Figure 4-1 Rated Switching Capacity of Three-Phase On-Load Tap-Changer (Iu, Ui)
4.2 Breaking Capacity
Breaking capacity (Pstmax) is the maximum switching capacity under load that can be safely interrupted. 
According to IEC 60214-1:2014, the breaking capacity must be at least twice the rated switching capacity (Pstn), 
i.e., twice the maximum rated through-current (Ium) at the relevant rated step voltage (Ui).
4.3 Short-Circuit Withstand Capability
According to IEC 60214-1:2014 , all continuously current-carrying contacts of the OLTC must withstand a short-
circuit current for 2 s (±10%) without welding, deformation, or mechanical damage. The initial peak current must 
be 2.5 (±5%) times the RMS value of the rated short-circuit current. Refer the short circuit test current values to 
Table 1.

4.4 Operating Conditions
1. The operating temperature range for the OLTC in oil is -25 °C to +105 °C.
2. The ambient temperature range for the OLTC installation location is -25 °C to +40 °C.
3. After installation on the transformer, the verticality of the OLTC to the ground should not exceed 2%.
4. The installation location must be free of heavy dust and explosive or corrosive gases.
Note: For more severe environmental conditions for the OLTC or MDU, contact Huaming's technical department
for confirmation.
4.5 Voltage Gradient Across Components of the Selector Switch
a1 = Between tap selector contacts (connected or unconnected) at any tap position.
a = Between the start and end of the tap winding. If a coarse tap winding is present, it is also 
between the start and end of the coarse tap winding.
Note: When the coarse tap is in the Change-over selector (-) position: The permissible withstand 
voltage value "a" must be observed between the end of the coarse tap winding connected to the 
tap selector K-contacts and the tap selector contacts located at the end of the same tap winding, 
when subjected to impulse voltages.
b = Between tap selector contacts of different phases, and also between Change-over selector 
contacts of different phases. This gap is connected to the start (or end) of the tap winding or to 
one of the tap selector contacts.
The permissible voltage between the delta-connected tap changer contacts varies with the 
position of the Change-over selector and the tap selector. Therefore, different withstand voltage 
values for b1, b2, and b3 must be observed for this insulation gap:
b1 = Between selected contacts of different phases;
b2 = Between the selected contact of one phase and the unselected contact of another phase;
b3 = Between unselected contacts of different phases.
f = Between tap selector contacts and Change-over selector (+) contacts to ground.
Two values are used for the delta connection design:
f1 = Between the terminals and ground when the Change-over selector is in the (+) position, and 
between the Change-over selector (+) contacts and ground;
f2 = Between tap selector unselected contacts when the Change-over selector is in the (-) position, 
and between the Change-over selector (+) contacts and ground.
Additionally, when the coarse tap winding is located in the (+) position of the Change-over selector:
c1 = Between the (-) contacts of the Change-over selector and the terminals of the same phase.
c2 = Between the (-) contacts of the Change-over selector of a different phase, and between the (-) 
contact of the Change-over selector and the (+) contact of the Change-over selector of the other 
phase.
The following different permissible values are used for the delta-connected switches:
c21 = Between the Change-over selector (-) contact and the (+) contact of the Change-over selector 
of the other phase;
c22 = Between the Change-over selector (-) contacts of different phases.

4.7	 Insulation Level to Ground
The insulation level to ground is defined as the insulation between the live parts of the OLTC and grounded 
components. It is determined by the AC power-frequency one-minute voltage withstand test and the impulse 
voltage withstand test. It is related to the location of the transformer tap winding, the regulation range and 
method, the winding connection method and arrangement, and the rated voltage of the transformer windings. 
It is determined by the insulation level to ground of the transformer regulating winding.
The insulation level to ground of the OLTC must be selected from the standard values specified in IEC 60214-
1:2014 and GB/T 10230.1-2019 (see Table 4-2) based on the maximum operating voltage (Um) of the equipment, 
using the lowest possible insulation value to meet the entire operating range.

5. Special Designs
Potential Connection of Regulating Winding
In on-load tap-changing transformers with high voltage ratings and large regulating ranges, the regulating 
winding is momentarily electrically disconnected from the main winding during Change-over selector operation, 
entering a "floating" state. At this time, the regulating winding will acquire a new potential, determined by 
the coupling capacitance to ground (Ce) and the coupling capacitance to adjacent windings (Cw) (see Figure 
5-2). This potential is typically different from the regulating winding potential before the Change-over selector 
operation. The potential difference between the two is called the offset voltage, which appears as a recovery 
voltage at the Change-over selector contact break. When the offset voltage reaches a critical value, it can cause a 
discharge at the Change-over selector, generating a large amount of gas, which is a serious problem. Therefore, 
if the OLTC offset voltage exceeds 15 kV (RMS), the potential connection of the regulating winding must be 
considered to prevent discharge during Change-over selector operation.
To facilitate Huaming's determination of the load on the Change-over selector and the size and number of 
potential Grading ring resistor elements, the following transformer parameters must be specified during OLTC 
selection:
1.	All transformer performance parameters: rated capacity, rated voltage, voltage regulation range, winding 
connection type, and insulation level;
2.	Relative arrangement of windings: the relative position of the regulating winding with respect to adjacent 
windings or winding components;
3.	The voltage at the terminals adjacent to the regulating winding and the electrical position of that coil in the 
transformer winding;
4.	Capacitance between the regulating winding and adjacent winding components;
5.	Capacitance between the regulating winding and ground or adjacent grounded winding (if present);
6.	Impulse load across half of the regulating winding;
7.	Operating and power-frequency test voltages across half of the regulating winding.

7. Tap Changer Controller
7.1 SHM-K Remote Digital Controller
The SHM-K remote digital controller (hereinafter referred to as SHM-K) can be used with the MDU for manual 
and automatic control of the OLTC. The SHM-K features a color, high-resolution touch screen with a 400 MHz 
main processor, 256 MB Flash memory, and 64 MB RAM, providing fast operation and large memory capacity. It 
communicates bidirectionally and digitally with the local control module via fiber optic cable.
SHM-K Remote Digital Controller Technical Parameters:
	
Operating Voltage: 85 V to 264 V (AC), 50/60 Hz; 100 V to 250 V (DC)
Power Consumption: Approx. 7 W
Maximum Number of Display position : 107
Operating Environment: Indoor, 0 °C to 50 °C; Relative Humidity < 95% non-condensing
7.2 HMC-3C On-Load Tap Changer Position Indicator
The HMC-3C on-load tap changer position indicator can be used with the MDU's remote position indication 
circuit to remotely display the tap position. It also provides "1-N", "Stop", and "N-1" functions, outputs BCD code 
position signals, and includes a remote control indicator.
HMC-3C Monitor Technical Parameters:
Operating Voltage: 220 V (AC)
Operating Frequency: 50 Hz
Maximum Number of Display position: 107
Operating Temperature: -10 °C to +40 °C
Note: Specify any other power supply technical requirements for the controller at the time of order.

8. Tap Changer Accessories
8.1 Bevel Gearbox
The bevel gearbox connects the horizontal shaft of the OLTC to the vertical shaft of the MDU, transmitting the 
drive torque to the OLTC. See Appendix Figure 25 for the external dimensions of the bevel gearbox.
The Protective relay is a protective device used in oil-immersed OLTCs. In the event of gas generation due to 
oil decomposition caused by an internal fault in the OLTC, or a surge in oil flow, the relay contacts actuate, 
connecting the designated control circuit and sending a signal or automatically tripping the transformer.
The Protective relay is installed in the connection pipe between the elbow at the top of the OLTC and the oil 
conservator, with the arrow on the relay pointing towards the oil conservator. Huaming offers various models of 
8.2 Protective Relay
Protective relays. See Appendix Figure 26 for the mounting dimensions of the Protective relay.
